# Decimal

You already know this one. Decimal is base-10: ten digits (0–9), and each
position is worth ten times its neighbor.

```
100  10  1
```

`347` means `3×100 + 4×10 + 7×1`.

Binary works exactly the same way — positional notation, digits times
place value, added up — it just uses two digits instead of ten, and each
position is worth *two* times its neighbor instead of ten.

```
decimal:   100  10  1     -> 347
binary:    4    2   1     -> 111 = 4+2+1 = 7
```

Once you see decimal and binary as the same trick with a different base,
hexadecimal (base-16) stops being scary too.

## Real world

- Everyday counting and arithmetic
- Human-readable output in every app
